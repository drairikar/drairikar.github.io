MSc thesis

Statistical Climate Downscaling with Neural Operators and Diffusion Models

MSc thesis, TU Delft, 2026. PyTorch, trained on the Snellius supercomputer. Read it in the TU Delft repository.

Coarse reanalysis such as ERA5 (~25 km) misses local structure, and simulating it at high resolution is expensive. The thesis treats downscaling from ERA5 to the CERRA reanalysis (~5.5 km) as an inverse problem: a diffusion prior trained on CERRA is combined with ERA5 through a learned forward operator (diffusion posterior sampling, DPS). A sequential prior that jointly models neighbouring states adds temporal structure.

  • Compared a U-Net baseline, neural operators, vanilla DPS and sequential DPS for 10 m wind and 2 m temperature over Central Europe and Iberia.
  • The U-Net has the lowest RMSE but is too smooth, and neural operators are smoother still. Both DPS variants keep fine-scale variability close to CERRA.
  • The sequential prior cuts temporal log-spectral distance by 1.5–4× against vanilla DPS and 2–15× against the U-Net at comparable point-wise accuracy. The exception is 2 m temperature over Iberia.

Advised by Justin Dauwels and Carlo Saccardi. The code is private until successful publication.

Distributed consensus: gossip vs. PDMM

Average and median consensus in a 150-node wireless sensor network, where each node talks only to its radio neighbours. Includes optimised gossip probabilities (via CVXPY), synchronous and asynchronous PDMM, and packet-loss experiments.

Asynchronous broadcast PDMM converges with 3.8× fewer transmissions than random gossip.

NumPy, CVXPY

Eye-blink removal from EEG

A multichannel Wiener filter for 19-channel EEG. It estimates the blink component from time-lagged covariances of the frontal electrodes and subtracts it, leaving neural activity largely intact.

NumPy, SciPy

Track-before-detect particle filter

Tracks a weak target from raw, unthresholded array scans and jointly infers which point-spread function produced the returns. The target amplitude is marginalised analytically in the likelihood.

Optimisation and inverse problems

Change detection in a time-varying AR model

Counting change points is a non-convex ℓ₀ problem. Relaxing it to a total-variation penalty gives a convex program, solved with CVX and with a projected subgradient method.

Recovers all three change points.

CVX, CVXPY, MATLAB

Wavefield imaging by inverse scattering

Reconstructs an object from the waves it scatters, using the Born approximation with 2-D Helmholtz Green's functions, multi-source and multi-frequency data, and Tikhonov or truncated-SVD regularisation.

1.7% reconstruction error without noise, 10% at 20 dB SNR.

MATLAB, NumPy
